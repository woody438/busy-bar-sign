What you're looking at

A visual mock of the wall display at true full-screen proportions — the clock is running on your machine's real time, and the sign is on an eight-second demo cycle. The detection is faked here. In the Mac app that bottom line is driven by CoreAudio, and the sign follows it.

The second ring

Sixty amber segments fill clockwise across each minute and reset at the top — the studio-clock convention, which is why there's no second hand. The leading segment fades up across its own second, so the sweep reads as smooth from across a room.

The LED module

Real seven-segment digits, drawn segment by segment with mitred ends — the unlit ones stay faintly visible the way they do on an actual module. Hours and minutes run large, seconds smaller on the same baseline, colon blinking at 1 Hz behind a smoked acrylic window.

Two legends, one lit

Both words are always on the panel; only one is powered. The dark one stays visible as engraved acrylic, the way a real on-air sign does. Switching strikes the lamp with a short flicker before it settles.

The bottom line

Live detection state — which app holds the microphone and whether the camera is up. Fed by kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunningInput, so dictation and music apps can be filtered out rather than lighting the sign.

Manual override

Auto-detection is the default, not the only option. A menu-bar item and a hotkey force the sign on or off — for an in-person meeting, a recording session, or an afternoon you'd rather not be interrupted.

Living on a wall

Built to run unattended: display sleep inhibited, a dim schedule after hours, and slow sub-pixel drift so nothing burns in. The bounce light behind the panel shifts with the state, like a real lamp throwing colour into the room.
