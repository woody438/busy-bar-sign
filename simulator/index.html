3840 × 2160 monitor · bar 3840 × 672 (31%) · 118 × 16 LEDs at 32 px

What you're looking at

The wall display re-imagined as a pixel LED bar, running on the same detection engine as the original. Everything on the bar is drawn LED by LED in the BUSY Bar's own pixel fonts, and the transitions are rebuilt from the frame sequences in its firmware. The clock is your real time; the call state here is a demo.

Why 118 × 16

Sixteen rows is what gives the type its chunk, so that stays. A real bar is 72 columns — too narrow to set "ON A CALL" beside a clock — so the panel runs wider. At 32 px per LED it lands exactly on a 4K screen's pixels, full width and 31% tall.

The status pill

The sign itself: a gradient pill in the firmware's exact reds and greens, with a pale rim and a slow sheen crossing it every eight seconds. It never drains or dims — on this display the status is the point.

A change of state

A shockwave bursts from the top edge, clears the old screen and floods the panel in the new colour. The status fills the whole bar in 14-row capitals for a few seconds, then draws back and the clock wipes in behind it.

The clock

Hours and minutes in the bold face from the BUSY pill, with tabular digits so nothing shuffles sideways. Seconds fill a bar along the bottom of the pill, and sparks drift up through the dark field so the panel is never static.

Glass and glow

Each LED is a rounded emitter with a dark gap; off LEDs stay faintly visible, as they do behind a smoked diffuser. The glow comes from scaling the light up smoothly rather than blurring, which the Mac app can do identically.

Same engine underneath

Call detection is unchanged from the original: CoreAudio tells us which app has the microphone, dictation apps are ignored, and the change is debounced so the bar never flickers.
