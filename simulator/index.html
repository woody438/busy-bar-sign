3840 × 2160 monitor · bar 3840 × 664 (31%) · 118 × 16 LEDs at 32 px

What you're looking at

The wall display rebuilt as a BUSY Bar, on the same call detection as the original. Every LED is drawn by the same engine the Mac app runs — the Swift port is checked against this page LED by LED. The pixel fonts, colours, dot shape and transitions all come from the BUSY Bar's own open-source firmware. The clock is your real time; the call state here is a demo.

Why 118 × 16

Sixteen rows is what gives the type its chunk, so that stays. A real bar is 72 columns — too narrow to set "ON A CALL" beside a clock — so the panel runs wider. At 32 pixels per LED it lands exactly on a 4K screen, full width and 31% tall.

The status pill

A glossy pill in the firmware's exact reds and greens, with a slow sheen crossing it. The mic echoes the firmware's own on-call theme; the tick is the "available" mark every meeting app uses. It says "ON A CALL", not "ON CALL", which in British English means on standby.

A change of state

As on the device: the old screen presses down, a ring bursts from the top edge and floods the panel, and the new screen springs up. The status fills the whole bar in 14-row capitals for a few seconds, then the pill draws back and the time slides in beside it.

The clock

Set as the firmware's clock app sets it: bold 7-row digits in white on black, the colon dimming on odd seconds, and the day beneath at half brightness.

LEDs and glass

Dots follow Flipper's own preview shader — rounded squares at 85% of the pitch, shading toward their corners — and unlit LEDs vanish into the glossy black face. Colours are corrected for the firmware's LED gamma, so dim tones sit back the way they do on the device.

Same detection underneath

Unchanged from the original: CoreAudio says which app has the microphone, dictation apps are ignored, and changes are debounced so the bar never flickers. A menu-bar item can force either state.
